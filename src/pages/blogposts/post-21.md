There's an uncomfortable contradiction inside me, that's been there, known about, for years. I've turned that contradiction into cognitive dissonance. And this contradiction, historically, had a contradictory role, too. Being both a symptom and sign of depression ahead, something to avert and caution away from, as well as something that could leave me stranded in that sea of loneliness and depression, a sign of my life raft's danger.

It's funny because, I haven't really learned anything new recently, but it still feels like this knowledge that has been inside me forever is new. I've looked at it multiple times in the past few years. And I'm not being abstract or roundabout here, I swear. I've thought this, I've known it to be true, but in cowardness, in vanity, in ego, I've chosen to look away. To allow it to live in my peripheral vision but never to look right at it. What else is there to do?

Since I was 16, I wanted to be a teacher. I wanted to be a teacher not really for the most common reasons, I might say those are true too, but primarily because I devoted myself, all of myself, to the study and practice of ethics. Since then, I've given all of myself up in the pursuit of the most ethical ways to live, and no, that doesn't quite mean self-sacrifice, because I don't find self-sacrifice to be the most ethical way to live. Self-sacrifice is a risk of non-survival. And for me, the most ethical way to live is to live, truly live in the most full way that can be, but never as an individual, always as a community. For me, ethical living meant strong relationships and strong allies, but it also meant organizing one's life towards love, towards care. Ethical living meant living a life of care.

Teaching appeals to me because it allows me to teach the theory and ideas that I genuinely think encourage ethical, liberatory living; I do truly believe that theory is liberatory for those of us faced with hegemonic violence. But I also came to the conclusion that teaching is not praxis. It's harm reduction, at best. I accepted, in theory at least, that _teaching cannot be the center of liberation._ So I told myself, it's the path that would make me happy, or that it's still the best career, since you have to have one.

But to go through the heart of empire, the academy, which, as so many wiser individuals have told me, is an inherently traumatizing experience, to devote so much of my life that devours me such that I cannot do anything accept give myself to this empire and never truly create networks of care, beyond, if I am lucky, my own home, all so that I may then have what _must_ amount to no less than a side gig, a thing I do that is not me that I do only to pay the bills and survive, yet will tell me and teach me that what it is is truly the liberatory work, to allow it all to consume me and be spit out as something different and changed, to touch hearts with empire and wreck all the devastation and corruption that may take place?

If I am lucky, I will learn nothing from the experience. Have I already been lost? Have I already spent too much time melding with the thoughts of empire? Have I already given too much of myself up? Am I already losing the will to care?

And yet, when I think about this, and I think about dropping out, or I think about extending that gap year into perpetuity, I feel a bit of embarrassment. Not quite shame, but embarrassment. I think about telling people I work as a food service worker. And sure, it pays the bills, but they kind of pity me. The smart one, who was meant to go on and get that beautiful privileged PhD, probably end up working in the academy the rest of her life. Look at her, her career is so little. She is so little.

But isn't career as the center of one's life one of the very problems of our normative working culture? The contradictions roll in. I have read and learned and believed that as Graeber says, we must work less to save the planet, and what Spade says, liberation can never arrive in the form of a wage or a salary, to never allow the non-profit industrial complex to swallow you whole and feast on your love. And yet, I think every day about how to further my applications to request the academy swallow me whole and feast on my curiosity, and how perhaps to feed it my love, too. I work so hard, everyday, giving up all of the precious hours of my day to this empire's lapdogs so that one day I too may become one of them, and continue to give up all my hours in a fantasy of liberation. Because surely, one day, this work will create liberation? Maybe one day, someone will read these words and feel inspired enough to actually create change, to actually create the networks of care and create the new world in the shell of the old. Maybe one day, I'll have a student who will follow in my footsteps, and, inevitably, also be swallowed up into the academy. And I'll be so proud.

I wrote that imagination is the first work of liberation, and I think dreams are, in that vein too. But I'm suddenly realizing that dreams are also a predatory creation of empire. We are taught to dream properly. Dream about getting that sweet 9–5 120k salary job, enough to buy a house, raise some kids, and retire with your spouse. Or be one of those liberal dreamers. Dream about starting a non-profit charity organization, helping out needy people across the ocean who really, really appreciate the generosity of us privileged white folks. Or if you're _really radical_, dream about teaching a bunch of oppressed kids why they are oppressed, make them angry, teach them to do what you never did, or maybe just to do what you did. Dream about having a good job, whatever good means to you.

Why are our careers the locus of our dreams? Why is it that, if I were to work as a Barista the rest of my life, I would probably be seen by the normative eye to be dreamless? Okay, there's plenty of answers I could give. I don't actually care to answer that. The question I really care about, is why have I allowed the normative dream to overwhelm me? Why is my dream not to work as little as possible, only to spend my time writing up new worlds, coordinating networks of care, and participating in resistance against power? Why is my dream not to care and be cared for?

The truth is, I'm a coward. I am afraid to choose this non-normative dream. I'm far too vain to allow myself to create a dream that is no longer empty on the inside and vibrant on the outside, I'm too vain to look like nothing on the outside only to have a true well of imagination on the inside. My ego is too great. The fantasy, the dream, of the Great Man has truly engulfed me. I yearn to be great. I yearn to be admired. And the truth is, I'm going to have to learn to be okay with only being loved.
