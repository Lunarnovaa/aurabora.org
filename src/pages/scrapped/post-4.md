I constantly am hearing people use the terms of "Assigned Female at Birth" and "Assigned Male at Birth" in cases where they should not be used, and it's bothering me. So here I am to complain, explain, and hopefully have a writing to send people when they misuse these terms.

## A basic overview of the term "Assigned Gender at Birth"

**Assigned Gender at Birth (AGAB): The gender an authority figure states that a person is at birth.**

So, when I was born (technically much before but for the sake of discussion let's say it was in fact at my birth), the doctor erroneously stated that I am a boy, hence, I am "AMAB" (Assigned Male at Birth).

Now, why does this term exist? In queer communities I have heard this term discussed and ultimately, its only purpose is the following: to be inclusive of intersex and transgender people whose gender does not conform to the one they were raised as.

In practice, this means that the terms AMAB and AFAB are useful insofar as they allow people to discuss how children are socialized/conditioned in regards to gender without being exclusive to intersex and transgender people. For example, I may express that as an AMAB person, I was oddly expected to be interested in sports, despite my consistent and repeated rejection of such matters. An AFAB person may even rely upon their childhood experience of gender socialization/conditioning to describe cultural misogyny regarding the disturbing way our society often treats female (as in the gender) children.

Let me be clear: these terms do not, should not, and, by their intended purpose, **cannot** refer to bodies. There is no such thing as an "AMAB" or "AFAB" body.

## Why defining an AGAB body is actually not based and is in fact a horrific abuse of the term

To understand this, we have to really understand the history of this term. As cisnormative definitions of bodily sex broke down and the existence of intersex people became recognized, it became increasingly obvious that, well, gender isn't "physical." Doctors would commonly [mutilate]() intersex children with "corrective" surgery to force them to conform to binary sex. This is because not all bodies conform to cisnormative, binary sex---as we now know, sex is really much more of a [binomial distribution](). So, AGAB was proposed in order to help intersex people define their existence outside of bodily sex. In essence, its existence is to be exclusively speaking to cultural enforcement of gender.

Furthermore, as explained above, AGAB is used extensively by trans people to define themselves without suggesting that they "used to be" a different gender, as many trans people do not see themselves that way. This means that a fully transitioned trans man who has gotten bottom and top surgery and has been on hormones for years to the extent where their bodily sex is closer to male than female may still refer to themself as AFAB (because they were assigned female at birth).
